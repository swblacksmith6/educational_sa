Executive Summary

Pace Digitek is a Bengaluru-based telecom passive-infrastructure EPC firm that built its ₹2,400 cr revenue base on the BSNL 4G tower deployment and is now mid-pivot into utility-scale Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) manufacturing, EPC, and Build-Own-Operate assets for state DISCOMs and central PSUs. The investment case rests on its ₹11,338 cr order book (78% energy) and ₹4,433 cr of BOO capex pipeline yielding 12-year annuity revenues, anchored by the live MSEDCL 750 MW project.

Bear Case (Top 5)

The strongest arguments AGAINST owning

  1. Cash never reaches the bank. 3-year cumulative CFO ≈ ₹0 against ₹525 cr cumulative PAT. The BOO pivot is structurally cash-negative until annuity collections begin in FY28.
  2. Pseudo-diversification. Swapped one PSU customer (BSNL) for a stack of PSU customers (SECI/NTPC/NLC/MSEDCL/KPTCL/KREDL/DVC) — still 96%+ public sector exposure with L1 tender pricing risk.
  3. BOO economics are thin. Project IRR 10-11.5% at 9% cost of debt and 70% leverage. Recent BESS tenders bid at ₹1.7 lakh/MW/month vs Pace's ₹2.19-2.55 lakh — 20%+ tariff compression would push equity IRR below cost of capital.
  4. Simultaneous execution risk on 5 fronts. 2.5→10 GWh mfg + 75 MSEDCL sites + SECI ₹1,159 cr EPC + 4 BOO projects + container backward integration — all in 12 months. Probability of slippage is high (already 1-2 quarter slip in container fab and 5 GWh commissioning).
  5. Standalone parent is shrinking. Standalone revenue fell 24.7% YoY in FY26 (₹1,711 cr vs ₹2,271 cr). Consolidated growth comes from intercompany sales to BOO SPVs / Lineage that get eliminated. Real underlying business momentum unclear.

Management Credibility Rating: MEDIUM-LOW (provisional)

Key takeaway from concalls

Pace cleanly met its first-ever year of listed guidance on revenue and PAT (₹2,641 cr / 11.4% PAT margin), and built an enviable ₹11,338 cr order book in its BESS pivot. But every granular operational milestone has slipped by 1-2 quarters, working-capital and cash-conversion problems remain unresolved, and analyst grilling on BOO unit economics revealed the headline "85% EBITDA" claim collapses to ~10% PAT after depreciation and interest. Management is delivering at the financial top-line but the operational walk has lagged the talk; second year of listed disclosures (FY27 quarterlies) is the real credibility test.

Top 3 risks (what can break the thesis)

Risk to thesis

  1. Cash-flow / working capital trap deepens. Receivables grew faster than revenue in FY26; 3-yr cumulative CFO ≈ ₹0. Without operating cash generation, BOO equity IRR claim is unverifiable.
  2. BESS tariff compression. Recent state tenders at ₹1.7 lakh/MW/month vs Pace's ₹2.19-2.55 lakh. 20% tariff cut pushes equity IRR below cost of capital.
  3. Concentration risk. 96% PSU + 94% telecom (FY25); in FY26 it's the same systemic exposure just spread across more PSU counterparties. Standalone parent revenue down 24.7% in FY26.

Verdict

2 HIGH-conviction triggers (MSEDCL annuity already commissioned + Energy OB signed with named PSUs) + 1 MEDIUM (mfg capacity on track) + 2 OPTIONALITY upsides (HoldCo + exports). Credible re-rating story IF cash conversion turns positive in FY27 and BESS execution stays on calendar. Working-capital evidence and operational milestone slippage so far suggest the multiple won't expand until investors see one clean year of BOO annuity collections. Best treated as a WATCH with a re-look at Q2 FY27 results.
