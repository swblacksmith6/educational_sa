## Step 2 — 12-Point Annual Report / RHP Analysis

### 1. What does the company do? (Plain English)
Pace Digitek is a **Bengaluru-headquartered telecom passive infrastructure and energy-storage (BESS) EPC + manufacturing house**. It started in 2007 as a power-electronics manufacturer for telecom towers (SMPS, IPMU, hybrid DC systems via the *Lineage Power* brand acquired from GE in FY14). Over time it expanded into turnkey telecom tower & optical fibre (OFC) projects (notably the BSNL 4G Saturation Project — ₹7,568 cr value, 9,595 sites) and from FY24-25 has pivoted hard into **battery energy storage** — manufacturing containerised lithium-ion BESS at Bidadi, Karnataka and executing utility-scale BESS projects on EPC, supply-only and Build-Own-Operate (BOO) models. (RHP p.274; concalls)

## Step 5 — Bear Case (5 strongest arguments AGAINST owning)

1. **CFO has been negative in 2 of last 3 fiscal years** — accounting profits have not translated to cash. 3-year cumulative CFO is virtually zero against ₹525 cr of cumulative PAT. The 4G Saturation Project (BSNL) created the ₹2,400 cr revenue base but trapped working capital in receivables. With BESS BOO pivot, the cash drain is **structurally permanent** (12-yr payback projects funded by 70% IREDA debt + IPO equity).

2. **Public-sector tender concentration (96% of revenue) + telecom (94%) is a single-bucket bet.** The new BESS order book is *also* dominated by state DISCOMs (MSEDCL, KPTCL), state generators (MAHAGENCO, KREDL) and central PSUs (SECI, NLC, NTPC, DVC). Switching ends from BSNL towers to BESS PSUs doesn't reduce concentration risk — it's the same single-buyer dynamic, just in a new sector.

3. **BESS BOO economics are razor-thin.** Management openly stated project IRR is 10-11.5% and equity IRR ~14% — at 9% cost of debt with 70% leverage and 12-year payback, this is not a "great" return profile. With Q3 FY26 commentary about aggressive bidding (₹1.7 lakh/MW/month vs Pace's quoted ₹2.19-2.55 lakh range), tariff compression is a clear and present risk.

4. **The whole investment case rests on flawless execution of a brand-new capability.** Pace had **₹0 BESS BOO revenue before FY26**. The MSEDCL project is the first-ever site commissioned (Sep 2025). They are simultaneously: (a) building 2.5→10 GWh manufacturing capacity, (b) commissioning 75 sites for MSEDCL, (c) executing SECI ₹1,159 cr EPC, (d) scaling 4 BOO projects, (e) backward-integrating container fabrication. The probability of a slippage / cost over-run somewhere is high.

5. **Standalone P&L deteriorating; consolidated growth is intercompany.** FY26 **standalone revenue fell 24.7% YoY** to ₹1,711 cr while consolidated grew 8.3% — the gap is intercompany sales to BOO SPVs / Lineage that get eliminated. This means the *manufacturing & EPC parent* is shrinking in real economic terms; growth is bookkeeping from selling-to-self for asset creation. Translation: ROCE has fallen from 37.9% → 14.3% (Q4 IP) and is likely to compress further as more BOO assets sit on the balance sheet pre-COD.

## Step 6 — Verdict

# 🟠 **WATCH** (revisit on trigger)

**Justification:**
Pace Digitek is a credible mid-cap pivot story — strong promoter track record, well-timed IPO, real BESS manufacturing capability ahead of competitors, and a visible ₹11,338 cr order book (Energy 78% of OB). FY26 numbers landed almost exactly on guidance, which is a positive credibility signal. **However, the cash-conversion problem (CFO/PAT near zero), public-sector concentration shift from BSNL to BESS PSUs, sub-15% equity IRR on the central BOO thesis, and the simultaneous capex + receivables + inventory + leverage build make this a "show me" stock rather than a buy.** Wait for: (a) one full year of MSEDCL annuity collections (FY28), (b) PAT margin sustaining at 11-12% with growing BESS mix, (c) standalone revenue stabilising, (d) D/E peaking at <0.6×.
