## STEP 2 — 12-Point Annual Report Analysis

**(1) What the company does (plain English).** Power Mech is a Hyderabad-based engineering & construction contractor founded in 1999 by S. Kishore Babu (AR25 p.46). It started doing maintenance of coal/gas power plants, then expanded into Erection-Testing-Commissioning (ETC) of boilers/turbines/generators, Balance-of-Plant, civil works for power plants, long-term Operation & Maintenance (O&M), water/infrastructure (Jal Jeevan Mission, STPs, metro), and since 2020 into Mine Developer & Operator (MDO) coal-mining contracts (AR25 p.46–55). Essentially: a diversified power & infrastructure EPC + recurring O&M + mining-services play.

**(2) Business segments & revenue mix.** The company reports as effectively a single composite contracting business (no separate Ind AS operating-segment note found in FY25 financials). Management/MD&A disclosure: O&M revenue ~Rs 1,746 cr = **~34% of topline** (AR25 p.48). Per the brief's FY25 mix: Civil ~46%, O&M ~33%, Erection ~17%, MDO ~2%, Electrical ~1%. The strategic intent is to grow the high-margin, annuity-like O&M share (AR25 p.10–11).

**(3) Geography mix.** Predominantly domestic. International revenue ~Rs 232 cr in FY25 (~4–5% of sales) across nine countries — Saudi/Abu Dhabi, Oman, Kuwait, Qatar, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Nigeria, Senegal, Israel (AR25 p.11). Earnings in foreign currency note: Abu Dhabi Rs 162 cr, Nigeria Rs 68 cr the largest (AR25 p.192, Note 38). Management guides 25–30% growth for international over next few years.

**(4) Customer concentration.** Government/PSU-heavy. Clients: NTPC, BHEL, Coal India/CCL, SAIL, DVC, NLC, SCCL, KPCL, GMDC, SJVN, plus private utilities Adani Power, JSW, JSPL, Vedanta, Torrent (AR25 p.47–48). MD&A explicitly flags "Dependence on Government Projects … delays or policy changes can affect timelines and payments" (AR25 p.54) and "Client Concentration" as a risk category (AR25 p.55). Specific per-customer % not disclosed.

**(5) Promoter / management & pledging.** Sajja Kishore Babu — Founder, Chairman & MD; Mechanical Engineering graduate (Nagarjuna University), 30+ years in power sector (AR25 p.34). Family on board: Mrs. Sajja Lakshmi, Sajja Rohit, Sajja Anita. Independent directors include Vivek Paranjpe (ex-Group President HR, RIL) and Bontha Prasada Rao (former CMD of BHEL) — credible bench (AR25 p.34–35). Promoter holding 58.41% (Mar-26), down from 64.2% (Mar-23) due to FY24 QIP dilution. **No promoter pledge reported** — AR25 (p.~) states the company has not raised loans on pledge of securities. MD remuneration Rs ~11.48 cr (Board total, AR25 p.103+ remuneration note).

**(6) Capex cycle.** Stepping up sharply for MDO. Net fixed assets 189→231→323 cr; CWIP 3→11→**28** cr (Sep-25: 72 cr) — mine development underway. MDO commitments: Kotre Basantpur Pachmo (CCL/Coal India) Rs 9,294 cr/25 yr with Rs 366 cr planned infra investment; Tasra (SAIL) Rs 30,438 cr/28 yr with Rs 2,393 cr planned investment (AR25 p.51). Large equipment base incl. 300+ cranes (AR25 p.54). Capital commitments not provided for: Rs 85 cr (AR25 p.192, Note 35B).

**(7) Working-capital trend.** Debtor days crept up 91→90→102 (AR25 p.55, Key Ratios). Working-capital days 75/86/74. The headline issue is the FY25 **CFO collapse to Rs 1 cr** (from Rs 182–205 cr) — driven by MDO mine-development WIP/advances and inventory build (inventory days 11→14), not a margin problem. Current ratio fell 2.04→1.80; debt/equity rose 0.21→0.33 (AR25 p.55).

**(8) Debt & interest coverage.** Borrowings 477→396→735 cr (Sep-25: 960 cr), rising to fund MDO capex/WC. Interest coverage healthy at **5.98x** (FY25) vs 5.11x (FY24) (AR25 p.55). D/E still conservative at 0.33. Bank guarantees outstanding Rs 1,661 cr, fully **backed by pledged Fixed Deposit receipts** (AR25 p.192, Note 36) — i.e. cash-collateralised, low contingent risk.

**(9) Key risks disclosed.** MD&A lists: high competition / margin pressure; dependence on government projects (payment delays); raw-material (steel/cement) cost volatility; regulatory/environmental (esp. mining); international/political-currency risk; manpower/labour; safety & compliance; technology obsolescence; client concentration (AR25 p.54–55). Also flags US tariff/trade issues under "Economic Slowdown" (AR25 p.54).

**(10) Related-party transactions.** Largely intra-group with controlled mining/project SPV subsidiaries. FY25-26 proposed RPTs: **Kalyaneswari Tasra Mining Pvt Ltd** (74% sub) civil contracting up to Rs 1,500 cr (= 38.21% of consolidated turnover) plus Rs 400 cr guarantees/loans; **KBP Mining Pvt Ltd** (74% sub) civil works up to Rs 300 cr (9.55%) (AR25 p.~ notice, RPT explanatory statement). These are the MDO mine SPVs — project-specific, arm's-length (cost-plus/market), board & audit-committee approved. Other subsidiaries: Power Mech Industri (Noida workshop, backward integration), Power Mech SSA Structures, Aashm Avenues, Hydro Magus, GTA Power Mech (Nigeria/Dubai JVs), MAS Power Mech Arabia, PM Green Energy. AOC-1 (AR25 p.~268) shows most subs small; Nigeria (Power Mech BR FZE) PAT Rs 30 cr, Kailash River Bed Minerals LLP PAT Rs 36 cr — profitable; mining SPVs (Tasra Rs 2.2 cr PAT, KBP Rs -0.4 cr) still ramping.

**(11) Auditor changes / qualifications (3 yrs).** FY23 auditor **K.S. Rao & Co** (term ended at 25th AGM). From FY24 onwards **Brahmayya & Co** (FRN 000513S) appointed as statutory auditor (AR25 p.~ governance; FY24 AR notice). This is a routine rotation, not a contentious resignation. **All three years carry clean, unqualified "true and fair" opinions** (AR25 p.~127 audit report; FY23/FY24 audit reports). No qualifications, no Section 143(12) fraud reporting (FY23 AR p.~). Cost auditor M/s MPR & Associates.

**(12) Strategy & guidance (Chairman/MD letter).** Themes (AR25 p.10–11): (a) execute the record Rs 53,994 cr order book — "10x annual revenue, runway for sustained multi-year growth"; (b) tilt mix toward **high-margin, annuity O&M** for stable cash flows; (c) scale GCC/West-Africa international 25–30%; (d) ride Jal Jeevan Mission (extended to 2028), FGD, renewables, water; (e) "financial prudence through cost control and **working-capital optimisation**" (explicit acknowledgement of the WC pressure); (f) evolve "from construction contractor into a trusted partner for cleaner/greener future." No hard numeric revenue guidance beyond the international growth band and order-book conversion.

## STEP 5 — Bear Case (5 hard-hitting points)

1. **Cash conversion has broken down.** FY25 CFO was Rs 1 cr against Rs 348 cr PAT and FCF was -Rs 159 cr. A contractor that doesn't convert profit to cash is funding growth with debt (borrowings 396→735→960 cr) — if the MDO WC unwind takes longer than guided, the balance sheet thins fast.

2. **MDO execution & funding risk.** The Rs 9,294 cr (CCL) + Rs 30,438 cr (SAIL/Tasra) contracts are 25–28 year, capex-heavy mining ventures the company has never operated at scale. Land/environmental clearances, coal off-take pricing, and ~Rs 2,760 cr of planned investment carry execution, regulatory and financing risk; the SPVs are still near breakeven.

3. **Government/PSU receivable risk.** Customer base is heavily NTPC/Coal India/SAIL/DVC/state utilities; debtor days already crept to 102. Management itself flags "Dependence on Government Projects" and "Client Concentration" (AR25 p.54–55). Budget cuts or payment delays from a few PSUs hit cash directly.

4. **Structurally thin, competitive margins.** OPM stuck at ~11–12% in a fragmented contracting/O&M market (MD&A flags "High Competition … lower profit margins"). Even the "high-margin" O&M push only nudges blended margins; this is not a pricing-power business, and steel/cement cost spikes compress further.

5. **Order-cancellation & valuation re-rating risk.** The reported WBSEDCL BESS order cancellation of ~Rs 1,563 cr (Mar-2026) shows order-book fragility — a 10x book is only worth its conversion rate. With the stock having ranged Rs 1,718–3,415 (a 2x swing) and P/E at ~24x TTM, a single growth/cash-flow disappointment could trigger a sharp de-rating from current levels.

## STEP 6 — Verdict

### WATCH

Power Mech is a well-managed, clean-accounting contractor (unqualified audits, no pledge, trivial contingents, credible board) with a genuine 10x order book and an improving O&M-led margin mix — the franchise quality is real and the DuPont trend is healthy. However, the FY25 cash-flow collapse (CFO Rs 1 cr, FCF -Rs 159 cr), rising debt, and a Piotroski of 3/9 are all symptoms of an unproven, capex-heavy MDO bet whose payoff is years away and whose RPT structuring is large — too much is riding on flawless mine execution and a working-capital reversal to underwrite the current ~24x P/E with conviction. It earns a WATCH (not a PASS, because nothing here is broken; not a BUY, because the cash-conversion thesis is unproven and the valuation leaves no margin of safety) — re-rate to BUY-WORTHY if 1–2 quarters show CFO normalising and MDO mines moving to production.

### Follow-up questions for deeper dive
1. What is the **quarterly trajectory of CFO and net working capital** through FY26 — is the FY25 dip reversing, or is the WC drag structural to MDO operations?
2. **MDO ramp status:** has either Kotre Basantpur or Tasra reached first coal / commercial production, and what are the actual vs planned investment and IRR assumptions on these SPVs?
3. What was the financial/margin impact of the **WBSEDCL BESS Rs 1,563 cr cancellation**, and how much of the Rs 53,994 cr order book is similarly cancellable or slow-moving (e.g. JJM extensions)?
4. **Receivables ageing and PSU-specific exposure** — how concentrated are the Rs ~1,040 cr receivables, and what is overdue beyond 180 days?
5. How is the rising debt (Sep-25 Rs 960 cr) being **termed-out and funded** — and is another equity raise likely to fund MDO capex (further dilution)?
6. What are the **actual blended margins and contribution of O&M vs Civil vs MDO** segment-by-segment, and is the high-margin O&M mix-shift actually showing up in consolidated OPM?
