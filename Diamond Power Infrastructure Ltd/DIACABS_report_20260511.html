Executive Summary

Diamond Power Infrastructure is India's oldest single-location cables and conductors manufacturer (Vadodara, 110 acres), reset under NCLT in June 2022 by a consortium of GSEC Ltd and Mr Rakesh Shah (Monarch Group). After two years of ramp-up, the company has turned profitable at scale, with a Q3 FY26 EBITDA margin of 14.7%, PAT of ₹49.7 cr (+693% YoY), and a disclosed order book of ₹3,500 cr+. The execution is real; the question is whether the share price (already at ~64× annualised PAT) leaves any margin of safety, and whether the credit rating (still "BWR D / Issuer Not Cooperating") will catch up with the operating turnaround.

Bear Case (Reproduced)

  1. Negative tangible net worth (-₹881 cr). The company is solvent only because the resolution applicant's SPV (Gomex Aviation) has bought back the ex-bank ₹1,900 cr 30-year, 0.001%-coupon bond. Conventional book value is deeply negative.
  2. "Issuer Not Cooperating" credit rating (BWR D). For a listed company with ₹2,800 cr of rated debt, this is a serious governance signal even if business performance has improved.
  3. Customer concentration on Adani Group. ~70% of ₹3,500 cr order book is Adani Group. Any pause or renegotiation by a single counterparty blows a hole in the FY27/FY28 thesis.
  4. Adani and Aditya Birla / UltraTech entering cables in 2026. Both have signalled large in-house cable manufacturing ambitions. Diamond is small (~₹1,600 cr revenue annualised) vs Polycab ~₹29,000 cr — limited pricing power.
  5. Valuation already discounts the turnaround. ~64× annualised PAT, ~45× FY28E PAT. Polycab is ~45× with 5× the revenue, 30% market share and clean balance sheet. ₹1,000 cr QIP will dilute the EPS further if priced sub-₹200.
