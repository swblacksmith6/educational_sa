## Step 2 — 12-Point Annual Report Analysis

### 1. What does the company do?
RIR Power Electronics (formerly Ruttonsha International Rectifier, founded 1969) designs and manufactures high-reliability power semiconductors (diodes, thyristors, IGBT-modules, bridge rectifiers, 28 mm – 125 mm devices up to 5,000 V) and power equipment (rectifiers, battery chargers, high-power stacks, AC voltage regulators) from a 12,000 sq m plant in Halol, Gujarat. The company is now executing a **₹618.6 crore Silicon Carbide (SiC) fabrication plant in Odisha** — India's first end-to-end SiC fab — based on process know-how licensed from related-party SiCamore Semiconductor Inc., USA. End-markets: Defence, Railways (RDSO Part-I supplier), Nuclear, Renewables, Oil & Gas, Mining, EV, Green Hydrogen electrolysers. (AR25 pp. 2–9, 45)

### 2. Business segments and revenue mix
Reported as **a single segment "Power Electronics"** per Ind AS 108 (AR25 p. 45). Management commentary discloses three product/channel groups: Semiconductor Devices, High-Power Equipment, and Exports — all three reportedly grew, with combined revenue growth of ~27%. Granular sub-segment revenue mix is NOT disclosed — a transparency gap that complicates monitoring.

### 3. Geography mix
Export House status awarded by Govt of India. AR25 highlights "300+ customers across the globe" and "10+ countries" but **does not publish domestic vs export % split**. Concalls indicate exports ~25-30% of revenue with a stated intent to scale international share materially.

### 4. Customer concentration
Top-customer concentration is **not disclosed** in the AR. Trade receivables ageing (Note in AR25) shows no obvious single-customer dominance, but the absence of explicit Top-5/Top-10 disclosure is a gap. Government / PSU clients (Indian Railways via RDSO, NPCIL Nuclear, Defence PSUs) appear to be material.

### 5. Promoter / management background and pledging
- Promoter holding: **61.68% at 31-Mar-2025** (down from 69.73% prior year — a **7.5 percentage-point drop** mainly Mrs. Bhavna H. Mehta selling). (AR25 Note 14.5)
- Chairman: **Dr. Harshad Mehta** (technologist; also Chairman of Silicon Power Corp USA and SiCamore — see related-party section).
- MD: **Mrs. Bhavna H. Mehta** (Promoter, Non-Executive turned MD).
- CEO & CFO: Mr. Ramesh G. Trasi (also receives ₹60.18 L consultancy charges — paid as KMP).
- **No pledged shares disclosed** by promoters.
- Board changes were heavy in FY25: three long-standing directors retired/resigned (Kisan Choksey, Pravin Shah, Piyush Shah); two new directors added (Sonali Mehta — family; Rajiv Choksey).

### 6. Capex cycle status — **AGGRESSIVE EXPANSION**
- **Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP) jumped to ₹29.29 crore** as of 31-Mar-2025 vs. negligible prior year. (AR25 Note 38)
- **Intangible Assets under Development (SiC process know-how): ₹42.99 crore** (Note 43).
- Total Odisha SiC plant outlay announced: **₹618.6 crore** (~10× FY25 net worth).
- FY25 cash capex outflow was ₹78.37 crore (per Cash Flow Statement) — funded primarily by ₹45.23 crore share-warrant proceeds.

### 7. Working capital trend
| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inventory days (COGS basis) | ~166 | ~161 | ~126 |
| Receivables days (Rev) | ~98 | ~98 | ~97 |
| Payables days | ~70 | ~59 | ~71 |
| Inventory turnover (×) | – | 2.26 | 2.89 |
| Trade receivables turnover (×) | – | 3.73 | 3.76 |
Working capital intensity has eased slightly in FY25 (inventory turning faster) but trade receivables still ~3 months, and **trade receivables ABSOLUTE rose 37% YoY** (₹26.49 Cr vs. ₹19.32 Cr) — faster than revenue (29%) — a yellow flag. (AR25 Note 47)

### 8. Debt position and interest coverage
- Total Borrowings 31-Mar-25: **₹11.99 crore** (all current/short-term). (AR25 Note 20)
- Net worth FY25: ₹112.19 crore (vs. ₹59.75 Cr FY24 — almost doubled via warrant conversions).
- **Debt / Equity = 0.13** (FY24: 0.18). (AR25 Note 47)
- Interest Coverage = **7.89×** (FY24: 8.00×) — healthy.
- BUT large SiC capex (~₹618 Cr) will likely need **significant fresh debt and/or equity** in FY26-FY28. Current balance sheet cannot fund it.

### 9. Key risks disclosed by management
Disclosed: input cost / forex volatility; supply chain / geopolitical (US-China, Ukraine, Middle East); global slowdown; tech disruption; new-plant execution & ramp-up risk on SiC; reliance on a few technology partners (SiCamore — related). Not explicitly disclosed but evident: customer concentration in PSU/defence orders; very high related-party-transaction footprint on the SiC IP purchase.

### 10. Related-party transactions — **MATERIAL & ELEVATED**
- **Purchase of SiC process know-how / intangible IP from SiCamore Semiconductor Inc. (USA): ₹41.95 crore in FY25**. SiCamore is described as "an entity under common control" — 80 % held by promoter group entity. Approved by minority shareholders 30-Sep-2024 with 99.88% approval. Independent valuer-certified arm's length. (AR25 Note 36.2 / Note 43)
- Plant & Machinery purchase from subsidiary Visicon Power Electronics ₹27.52 crore.
- Loan to / from Visicon (gave ₹0.77 Cr, repaid ₹9.62 Cr, outstanding nil at FY25 close).
- Other related-party items: rent to MD ₹8.40 L, consultancy ₹60 L (CEO/CFO), director sitting fees.
- **Post FY25 (29-May-2025): Board approved sale of 100% stake in Visicon Power Electronics to Silicon Power Corp USA** (another related party of Dr. Harshad Mehta).
- **Aggregate FY25 related-party flows ~₹70 crore — VERY HIGH relative to revenue ₹86 Cr.** Most of it is one-time IP purchase; recurring run-rate is much smaller. Investors must satisfy themselves that the SiC IP is genuinely worth ₹42 Cr; the independent-valuer + 99.88% minority approval is positive but does not eliminate the question.

### 11. Auditor changes / qualifications in last 3 years
- Statutory Auditors: **Kirtane & Pandit LLP** (FRN 105215W/W100057). Re-appointed at the 55th AGM (Sep-2024) for 5 years (till 60th AGM, FY30). No qualifications, reservations, or adverse remarks across FY23, FY24, FY25.
- Secretarial Auditor: M/s Neetu Agrawal & Co. appointed FY25 onwards.
- Internal Audit: Bhandarkar & Kale, CAs.
- No history of auditor switching or modified opinions. **Clean audit.**

### 12. Strategy and guidance stated by Chairman/MD
Dr. Harshad Mehta (Chairman) frames the company around three pillars: **(a)** core power semiconductor / equipment business growing in defence, railways, exports; **(b)** rectifiers for **green hydrogen** electrolysers as a growth vertical; **(c)** the **Odisha SiC fab** as the centrepiece transformation, positioning RIR on the global SiC value chain and addressing EV / renewables / aerospace / hydrogen. Quantitative guidance is not provided in AR25; concalls give more specific revenue / margin commentary.

## Step 5 — Bear Case (5 bullets)

1. **Massive related-party intangible.** ₹42 Cr paid to promoter-controlled SiCamore for SiC process IP, capitalised as an intangible. If Odisha plant slips, under-utilises, or yields underperform, this asset must be impaired — and minority shareholders foot the bill while the promoter group has already received the cash.

2. **CFO has not supported PAT for three years running.** 3-yr cumulative CFO/PAT ≈ 0.12. If Odisha capex stretches working capital further, the gap between "book profit" and "cash" widens, forcing repeated equity raises and warrant issuances that dilute optionality.

3. **Single-segment, no transparent disclosure.** Company reports one segment; sub-segment mix, top-customer share, geography %, order book, and SiC unit economics are not in the AR. Investors must rely on concall colour, which is light on hard numbers.

4. **Tiny company taking on outsized capex (₹618 Cr SiC fab vs. FY25 revenue ₹86 Cr).** SiC fabs are notoriously capital-intensive, technology-risky, yield-sensitive, and slow to ramp. RIR has no prior wafer-fab experience. Time-to-positive-EBITDA on Odisha is multi-year and assumes flawless execution + government incentives flowing on schedule.

5. **Governance optics.** Promoter holding stepped down 7.5 pp during the year (Bhavna Mehta sold). Multiple long-tenured directors exited. SiC IP purchase from common-control entity. Visicon sale to another common-control entity (Silicon Power Corp USA) at undisclosed terms. Each event in isolation is defensible; the cluster warrants monitoring of future RPTs and director independence.

## Step 6 — Verdict

### 🟡 **WATCH** — revisit after FY26 H1 results and Odisha milestone updates.

**Three-sentence justification:**
RIR has a real, decades-old, profitably-growing core semiconductor + power-equipment business with credible defence/railway/nuclear customers, and the SiC opportunity in Odisha is genuinely strategic to India's import-substitution story — making the upside narrative compelling. However, the company is mid-flight into a ₹618 Cr capex that is ~7× its annual revenue, funded partly via promoter-related IP purchase, on a balance sheet whose 3-year CFO/PAT is only 0.12 — meaning the risk of dilution, impairment, or execution slippage is non-trivial. The right behaviour is to WATCH: track Odisha trial-production updates, SiC offtake / order announcements, quarterly working-capital normalisation, and any further related-party flows; convert to BUY only when CFO/PAT normalises above 0.7 and Odisha shows revenue contribution, or PASS if related-party flows escalate or capex slips materially.
