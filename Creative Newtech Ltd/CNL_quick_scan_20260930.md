# Creative Newtech Ltd (CNL): Company Quick Scan
*As of 30-Sep-2026. CMP ₹1,104 (29-Sep-2026 close, Bajaj Finserv markets page). Mcap ≈ ₹1,658 cr.*
*Basis: **consolidated** unless marked. Statements are printed in ₹ lakhs and converted once to ₹ crore (1 cr = 100 lakh). Annual-report page numbers are not available (no printed footers after extraction), so citations are by note.*

## Step 1: The report
- **Annual reports:** FY26 (year to 31-Mar-2026), with FY25 and FY24 for trends.
- **Auditor:** Gupta Raj & Co. (FRN 001687N), partner Nikul Jalan. The opinion is unmodified; accounts were signed 14-May-2026.
- **Subsidiaries (AR26 consolidated audit report, Other Matters):** both Hong Kong subsidiaries are **not audited by the group auditor**. The opinion on them relies on the work of other auditors and on figures certified by management.
  - Creative Peripherals & Distribution Ltd (HK): revenue ₹16.8 cr, loss ₹0.31 cr.
  - Secure Connection Ltd (SCL, HK), which holds the Honeywell licence: revenue ₹297.7 cr, PAT ₹37.4 cr, 77.5% owned.
- **Typo in the audit report:** it gives SCL's total assets as ₹37.1 cr (3,713.55 lakh). AOC-1 shows ₹137.1 cr (13,713.55 lakh).

## Step 2: 12-point analysis

**1. What it does.** CNL is a Mumbai technology distributor, founded in 1992. It runs two segments:
- **Market Entry Specialist** (≈86% of revenue): it imports and distributes other companies' IT, imaging, gaming, surveillance, cybersecurity and memory products (Cooler Master, Lexar, Fujifilm/Instax, Samsung monitors, Corsair, Matrix, Sparsh, Dahua, Kaspersky, EIZO, PDRL drones). It sells to channel partners, large-format retail, e-commerce, system integrators and, increasingly, government-linked buyers.
- **Brand Business** (≈14%): mainly **Honeywell-licensed** air purifiers, IT accessories, home audio and structured cabling. These are contract-manufactured in China, Taiwan and India and sold across about 38 countries in APAC, the Middle East and Africa through SCL. It also includes a CyberPowerPC joint venture (the 49% associate) and, from May-2026, the company's own brand **WOZOYO**.

**2. Segment mix (₹ cr, AR26 note 37, consolidated)**

| Segment | FY25 (restated) | FY26 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand Business | 270.7 (15.3%) | 380.9 (14.1%) | +41% |
| Market Entry Specialist | 1,503.8 (84.7%) | 2,318.9 (85.9%) | +54% |
| Brand segment result | 93.6 | 126.3 (33% of brand revenue) | +35% |
| Market Entry segment result | 55.1 | 87.3 (3.8% of segment revenue) | +59% |

*Read-through:* the low-margin distribution arm grew faster than the brand arm, so the brand share of revenue **fell**. The segments were also **redefined in FY26**. AR25 reported EB / FMSG / FMCT / FMEG; FMSG, where Honeywell sat, was ₹385.4 cr in FY25. That breaks comparison with earlier years in exactly the year the brand business became the headline.

**3. Geography (AR26 note 23B)**
- India revenue rose from ₹678.4 cr to ₹1,797.1 cr (+165%).
- Outside-India revenue fell from ₹1,096.1 cr to ₹902.6 cr (−18%).
- So all of FY26's growth is domestic, while the overseas book shrank.

**4. Customer concentration.** None of the three annual reports discloses it. CRISIL does:
- **~60–65% of revenue came from one overseas customer** in FY24–FY25 (Dec-2025 rationale).
- This improved to ~23% in H1 FY26 and **~25% in FY26** (Aug-2026 rationale).
- The improvement came from adding domestic customers, and those customers came with extended credit (point 7).

**5. Promoters and management**
- Ketan Patel holds 56.20%; the promoter group holds 56.62% (unchanged). No pledge or encumbrance is disclosed in AR26.
- **Leadership churn:**
  - Purvi Patel ceased as Whole-Time Director on 11-Aug-2025 and became Chief Strategy Officer.
  - **CFO Abhijit Kanvinde left on 14-Apr-2026.** Ajit Thakur was appointed on 14-May-2026, the same day the FY26 accounts were approved, so the company had no CFO for the month of the year-end close.
- **Share count dilution:** 1.20 cr shares (start of FY23) → 1.26 cr (FY23) → 1.41 cr (FY24) → 1.50 cr (FY25). That is **+25% in three years**, including ₹80.1 cr of preferential warrants in FY24.
- The FY27 AGM proposes 2 lakh ESOPs (≈1.33%).

**6. Capex.** The business is asset-light: PP&E is ₹8.2 cr and all manufacturing is contracted out. Intangibles rose from ₹0.09 cr to ₹4.07 cr (ERP/CRM per the CFO on the May-26 call). Not material.

**7. Working capital: the core issue (₹ cr, consolidated)**

| Trade receivables | 147.0 | 237.8 | **565.1** |
| Receivable days (on revenue) | 31 | 49 | **76** |

- Receivables grew 138% against revenue growth of 52%.
- The **credit-loss provision is ₹0.77 cr, just 0.14% of the book.** About 97% of receivables are under six months old, which is reassuring on age, but the provision cannot be tested against a book that more than doubled in one year.

**8. Debt**
- Current borrowings rose from ₹69.5 cr to **₹324.2 cr**; long-term debt is about nil.
- **Net debt is ≈ ₹302 cr** (cash ₹5.4 cr plus other bank balances ₹16.8 cr, mostly margin money).
- CRISIL puts gearing at 0.84× (from 0.23×) and interest cover at 4.99× (from 7.16×).
- Management says ₹171 cr of the debt is MSME vendor finance via TReDS (May-26 call, p.17). The notes show one undivided line, "Loans repayable on demand from Others ₹324.2 cr", so that split is **not disclosed** in the accounts.
- Q1 FY27 interest cost was ₹7.63 cr against ₹3.01 cr a year earlier.

**9. Disclosed risks.** CRISIL names intense competition, thin margins (3.5–4.5%) and a moderate working-capital cycle. On the May-26 call, management said Middle East shipping disruption could cut Middle East brand sales to about 50% (p.28). Other risks: renewal of the Honeywell licence (now Mar-2027) and the ongoing Honeywell corporate split.

**10. Related parties (standalone note 36, ₹ lakhs → ₹ cr)**
- **Click Retail Pvt Ltd** (whole-time director Vijay Advani is on its board):
  - FY26 sales to it were ₹26.8 cr (FY25: ₹7.7 cr).
  - The **outstanding balance was ₹47.7 cr** (FY25: ₹2.2 cr). The balance exceeds the whole year's sales.
  - Consolidated "trade receivables from related parties" total only ₹32.4 cr, so part of this balance sits outside trade receivables. **Its nature is not disclosed.**
- Secure Connection Ltd (HK): purchases from it of ₹155.4 cr.
- World EMS: sales of ₹26.7 cr.
- Promoter loans: ₹3.4 cr taken and ₹3.5 cr repaid.
- The family entities (the "PG" companies) show no material flows.

**11. Auditor.** Gupta Raj & Co. throughout, with no qualification. Consolidated audit fees are ₹0.12 cr. The weak points are the **non-audit of the HK subsidiaries** by the group auditor and the absence of any audit of the Click Retail balance's nature.

**12. Strategy (Chairman's letter, AR26).** Quotes:
- "*By 2030, we aspire to become the first Indian consumer technology brand with a meaningful global presence.*"
- "*While Market Entry and Distribution will remain the foundation of our business, we will continue expanding our Brand Business…*"
- The letter reports "*PAT grew by 32.35% YoY to Rs. 70.29 cr*". That figure includes ₹8.42 cr belonging to minority shareholders; **owners' PAT is ₹61.9 cr**.
- The letter does not mention cash flow, receivables or debt.

- **Reading:** FY26 ROE improved **only through leverage.** Margin and asset turnover both fell.
- Asset turnover has now dropped for two years, from 4.76× to 2.89×. That is a capital-efficiency problem (a bloated receivable book), not a margin problem.
- The kind reading is that this is a one-time build-up for new government and surveillance channels. The unkind reading is that growth is being bought with credit.

## Step 5: Bear case
1. **The growth was financed, not earned.** Revenue rose by ₹922 cr, and receivables plus inventory plus supplier advances rose by ₹427 cr, funded by ₹255 cr of new short-term debt. Operating cash flow was −₹272 cr.
2. **The profit engine is outside the audit.** SCL (HK) earns ₹37.4 cr of the ₹70.3 cr PAT, pays no tax, is 22.5% owned by minorities, is audited by other auditors, and is described as "wholly owned" in the Aug-26 deck when AOC-1 says 77.5%.
3. **An opaque related-party balance.** ₹47.7 cr is outstanding from Click Retail (director-linked) on ₹26.8 cr of annual sales, and its nature is undisclosed. That equals ≈68% of FY26 PAT.
4. **The brand story is shrinking in relative terms.** Brand share fell from 15.3% to 14.1%. Q1 FY27 brand revenue grew only +8.6% YoY (₹64.7 cr → ₹70.3 cr) against "at least 50%" guidance. The Honeywell licence renews in Mar-2027 while Honeywell is splitting itself up.
5. **The valuation already pays for a brand company.** At ~25× trailing EPS and 17× EV/EBITDA, the market is paying brand-company multiples for a business where 86% of revenue is 2–4% margin distribution with negative free cash flow.

## Step 6: Verdict
**WATCH (leaning PASS).**
- Reported earnings are growing fast and the Honeywell franchise is real (~33% segment margin).
- But FY26 converted none of that into cash, the balance sheet re-levered sharply, and there are unresolved disclosure gaps: the Click Retail balance, SCL ownership, and the Infinova status.
- The stock trades at a premium that assumes these resolve.

**What would move it to BUY-WORTHY**
1. Q2 FY27 (Nov-2026) and Q3 FY27 (Feb-2027) balance sheets show receivables back toward the 13–14% of sales management promised (May-26 call, p.26), about ₹350–400 cr on a ~₹2,900 cr run-rate.
2. H1 FY27 operating cash flow is positive, and FY27 operating cash flow is at least 50% of PAT.
3. The Click Retail balance is explained and reduced below ₹10 cr in the FY27 half-year disclosures.
4. Honeywell licence renewal is announced before Mar-2027, with no increase in royalty terms.
5. SCL ownership is clarified, i.e. the 22.5% minority is bought in or explained.

**What would move it to PASS**
1. Receivables above ₹600 cr, or net debt above ₹400 cr, at Sep-2026.
2. CRISIL downgrade triggers are hit: interest cover below 3×, or any single customer above 40%.
3. The Honeywell renewal is delayed past Mar-2027, or the licence territory shrinks.
4. The Infinova acquisition is closed with equity issuance before cash conversion is fixed.
5. Another senior finance departure.
