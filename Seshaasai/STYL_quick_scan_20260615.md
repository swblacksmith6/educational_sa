## Step 2 — 12-Point Prospectus Analysis

**1. What the company does.** Seshaasai is a multi-location, technology-driven manufacturing and solutions provider serving primarily the banking, financial services and insurance (BFSI) sector. It makes payment instruments (debit/credit/prepaid/transit cards, metal cards, cheque leaves, QR codes), provides secure omni-channel communication & fulfilment (statements, print, e-PDF, email/SMS), and offers IoT/RFID solutions (tags, traceability, sensor ecosystems). It has ~3 decades of relationships with major banks.

**2. Business segments & revenue mix (FY26, per Q4 call).** Payment Solutions ~50% (47.5% in Q4), Communication & Fulfilment ~39%, IoT ~11%. (FY25 segmental: Payments ₹9,147M / Comms ₹4,345M / IoT ₹1,062M.)

**3. Geography.** Overwhelmingly domestic India; no material individual markets outside India disclosed. FY25 domestic product sales ₹13,474M of ₹14,632M.

**4. Customer concentration (FY25).** Largest customer 17.0%; Top 5 = 49.1%; Top 10 = 65.8%. BFSI = ~84% of revenue. **High concentration — key risk.** Mitigant: 7 of top 10 customers have >10-year relationships; ~97–98% of revenue is recurring per management.

**5. Promoters / pledging.** Founders since 1993; post-offer promoter group holds majority. **Zero promoter shares pledged** (Prospectus, "none of the Equity Shares held by our Promoters are pledged"). Directors lack prior listed-company directorship experience (disclosed risk).

**6. Capex cycle.** Expansion phase — ₹1,979M of IPO net proceeds earmarked for expanding existing manufacturing units (FY26–FY27). Capacity utilisation: Comms ~64%, IoT ramping from 4.8% (FY23) to ~55% (FY25).

**7. Working capital trend.** Net Working Capital Days rising: 74 (FY23) → 62 (FY24) → **95 (FY25)**. Net working capital ₹3,812M FY25. **Deterioration to watch.**

**8. Debt & coverage.** Net Debt ₹2,375M (FY25); Net Debt/EBITDA 0.64x (improving from 1.18x in FY23); Net Debt/Equity 0.37x. ₹3,000M of IPO proceeds used to repay borrowings — balance sheet materially de-levered post-IPO.

**9. Key risks disclosed.** Customer concentration; not exclusive supplier / no long-term volume commitments; government/PSU project exposure & penalties; dependence on manufacturing assets; input (paper, chips) supply & price; data-security/IT dependence; inability to trace certain old corporate records.

**10. Related-party transactions.** Recurring dealings with promoter-interested entities (Creative Formulation (India) Pvt Ltd, Dandelion Technologies, Bharat Trading Corp) — purchases, rent, software maintenance, advances, preference-share investments. Size modest but ongoing; monitor.

**11. Auditor.** Statutory auditor Vatsaraj & Co.; restated financials carry **no audit qualifications**. (No qualifications to give effect to.)

**12. Strategy / guidance.** Grow recurring revenue and wallet share, cross-sell across verticals, premiumization in payments (metal cards — patent granted), scale IoT/RFID as long-term engine, SIM-card division now contributing. 19 patent applications filed (5 granted).

## Step 4 — Piotroski (est.) & DuPont

**Piotroski F-Score ≈ 6–7/9** (estimate): positive ROA ✓, positive CFO ✓, lower leverage ✓ (Net Debt/Equity 0.52→0.37), higher gross margin ✓ (36.6%→41.8%), no dilution-for-cash ✓. Misses: CFO < Net Income in FY25 ✗, asset turnover fell ✗ (revenue dipped).

**DuPont (FY25), RoE 34.8%:**

- Net margin: **15.1%** (up from 10.8% FY24, 9.4% FY23) — the key driver
- Asset turnover: declined (revenue −6% on flat/larger asset base)
- Leverage: falling (de-levering)

Read-through: RoE is high and quality (margin-led, not leverage-led), but the FY25 RoE of 34.8% is *down* from 39.0% (FY24) because asset turnover slipped on the revenue dip. Margin expansion offset most of it. RoCE strong at ~32%.

## Step 5 — Bear Case (strongest argument against owning)

1. **The stock has already broken trust** — down ~37% from a ₹423 IPO inside 9 months. The market is pricing in concern, not euphoria; something is off versus the IPO narrative.
2. **Revenue is not growing.** FY24 → FY25 → FY26 revenue went ₹15,583M → ₹14,632M → ₹14,410M — two consecutive years of *decline*. The bull story rests entirely on margin expansion, which is finite.
3. **Customer concentration is severe and rising** — top 10 = 66%, largest single client 17%, ~84% BFSI. Loss or repricing by one large bank would be material, and the company is explicitly *not* an exclusive supplier with no long-term volume commitments.
4. **Working capital is deteriorating** (NWC days 62→95) and FY25 cash conversion weakened — margin gains aren't fully converting to cash.
5. **Commodity/structural earnings quality** — much of the core (cards, cheques, statements) is mature/regulated manufacturing exposed to paper/chip prices, PSU tender penalties, and the secular decline of cheques; IoT is promising but only ~11% of revenue.

## Step 6 — Verdict

### 🟡 WATCH (revisit on trigger)

Seshaasai is a **profitable, cash-generative, de-levered, unpledged, founder-run** business with genuine moats (3-decade bank relationships, ~97% recurring revenue, high entry barriers in card manufacturing) and a real optionality leg in IoT/metal cards. After a ~37% de-rating it now trades at a far more reasonable ~17–18x FY26 earnings versus 28x at IPO. **But** two consecutive years of revenue decline and rising working-capital intensity mean the growth thesis is unproven on the top line — the entire FY25–FY26 profit story is margin-driven, and margins eventually plateau. This is a WATCH, not a BUY: the quality is there, the price is more attractive, but you want evidence of **renewed revenue growth** (IoT scaling, new bank wins, metal-card/SIM ramp) before underwriting it.

**Upgrade to BUY-WORTHY trigger:** two consecutive quarters of double-digit YoY revenue growth + NWC days back below 80.
