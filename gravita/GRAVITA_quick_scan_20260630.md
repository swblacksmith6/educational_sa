## Step 2 — 12-Point Annual Report Analysis

**1. What the company does.** Gravita is India's largest organised lead recycler and a multi-metal recycling and value-added-products company. It collects scrap (lead-acid batteries, aluminium, plastic, rubber/tyres, and now copper) through a global procurement network, recycles it, and sells refined metals, alloys and value-added products. Founded 1992 by first-generation entrepreneur Rajat Agrawal (Jaipur).

**2. Business segments & revenue mix.** Verticals: **Lead** (flagship, ~70%+ of volume), **Aluminium**, **Plastic**, **Rubber/tyre**, plus newer **Turnkey recycling solutions**, **Copper** (via RMIL acquisition, Mar 2026) and **Lithium-ion** (6,000 MTPA Mundra plant commissioned FY26). Value-added products are ~42-45% of mix; management targets ~45-50%. Non-lead businesses targeted at 35-40%.

**3. Geography mix.** Operations across Asia (India, Sri Lanka), Africa (Ghana, Senegal, Mozambique, Tanzania, Togo), Europe (Romania), and Americas (Dominican Republic). India + overseas revenue roughly split, with India share rising as Mundra/Sarigam capacity ramps. Sells to 400+ customers across 37 countries.

**4. Customer concentration.** Diversified — 400+ customers, 250+ domestic across 22 states, 50+ overseas across 25 countries. No single-customer dependence disclosed. Low concentration risk.

**5. Promoter / management.** Promoter holding **59.27%** (FY25), **fully demat, zero pledge**. Led by Rajat Agrawal (Chairman & MD), Yogesh Malhotra (WTD & CEO), Sunil Kansal (WTD & CFO). Avg management experience 30+ years; 50% independent board; ESG committee created.

**6. Capex cycle.** Active **expansion phase**. ~₹372 Cr capex in FY26; planned capex ramps to FY29 (Vision 2030). Capacity expanding from ~1.46 lakh MTPA (existing verticals, FY25) toward **8,00,000+ MTPA by FY29**. Mundra lead capacity expanded by 80,300 to 1,45,100 MTPA; Li-ion plant commissioned.

**7. Working capital.** Improving cycle — driven by higher domestic scrap sourcing, efficient inventory management, and back-to-back hedging. Management repeatedly cites a shortening working-capital cycle and improving circularity.

**8. Debt / interest coverage.** **Net debt-free** as of FY25 after a ₹1,000 Cr QIP (oversubscribed 3.5x). External credit rating **AA-** (ICRA & India Ratings); subsidiary RMIL upgraded to **IVR AA/Stable** (Jun 2026). Comfortable coverage.

**9. Key risks (management-disclosed).** Commodity price volatility (lead/LME), FX, scrap availability, regulatory/import-licence dependence, acquisition integration.

**10. Related-party transactions.** No materially significant RPTs requiring shareholder approval; AOC-2 disclosures clean.

**11. Auditor changes / qualifications.** Statutory auditor: **Walker Chandiok & Co (Grant Thornton)** — no auditor change. **However, a QUALIFIED OPINION exists** relating to the FY24 treasury-share/SAR-scheme gain (₹20.67 Cr that should have flowed through employee benefit expense); carried into FY25 as a comparability qualification. No impact on Other Equity. See red-flag #6.

**12. Strategy / guidance (Vision 2030).** ~20-25% volume CAGR, sustain ROIC ~25%, ~30-35% profitability growth, value-added products 45-50%, non-lead 35-40%, renewable power 25-30%. Diversification into copper, rubber and steel.

## Step 4 — Piotroski F-Score & DuPont

**Piotroski F-Score: ~6-7/9** (estimate). Positive net income ✓, positive CFO ✓, rising ROA ✓ (until FY25 QIP-driven base inflation), lower leverage post-QIP ✓, higher current ratio ✓. Points docked for FY25 share issuance (QIP) and a moderating asset turnover during the expansion build.

**DuPont (ROE decomposition):**
- ROIC has stepped down from ~31% (FY24) to ~24-27% (FY25-26) — still well above cost of capital, but the QIP-enlarged equity base and capex-in-progress dilute near-term returns.
- ROE = Net margin (~7-8%) × Asset turnover (moderating during capex) × Leverage (falling, now net-debt-free) ⇒ ROE in the high-teens, down from low-20s pre-QIP.
- Trajectory: returns are temporarily diluted by the equity raise and capacity under construction; the Vision-2030 thesis is that volume + value-added mix re-accelerate returns back toward 25%+ ROIC.

## Step 5 — Bear Case (strongest argument against owning)

1. **Premium valuation.** 31.8x TTM P/E and 4.9x book for a commodity-recycling business whose ROIC is *declining* (31%→24%) leaves no margin of safety if growth slips.
2. **Commodity + FX cyclicality.** Earnings ride LME lead/aluminium prices and the rupee; hedging smooths but does not eliminate the cycle. A spread compression hits margins fast.
3. **Acquisition-led diversification risk.** Copper (RMIL ₹562 Cr), Europe tyre, lithium and proposed steel stretch management across many new verticals and geographies — integration and execution risk, plus goodwill.
4. **Accounting/governance blemish.** A standing qualified audit opinion (SAR/treasury) is a yellow flag on financial-reporting discipline at a company that markets itself on governance.
5. **Capital intensity & dilution history.** 8 lakh MTPA by FY29 needs sustained capex; the FY25 ₹1,000 Cr QIP shows the model can require external equity, diluting per-share compounding.

## Step 6 — Verdict

### 🟢 BUY-WORTHY (deep-dive next) — with valuation as the gating concern

Gravita is a structurally advantaged, net-debt-free compounder riding a genuine formalisation-of-recycling tailwind (EPR, BWMR, vehicle scrappage, GST), with a 25% 5-yr revenue CAGR, 49% EBITDA / 31% PAT 5-yr CAGR (FY21-26, per Q4FY26 concall), 24%+ ROIC, AA- rating, zero-pledge promoter at 59%, and a credible Vision-2030 roadmap. The quality clears the bar for a full deep dive. The two things to underwrite before buying are **(a)** whether the rich 31.8x multiple already prices the growth, and **(b)** execution on the copper/lithium/steel diversification. The lone RED flag is a technical accounting-presentation qualification, not a cash or fraud signal.
