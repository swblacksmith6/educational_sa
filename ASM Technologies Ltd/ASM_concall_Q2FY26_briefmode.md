## 2. Segment performance

Management does **not** disclose Hi-Tech vs Engineering split, or DLM/ER&D split by industry. Pushback from analysts on this was significant — Anant Jain (page 11) and Rupesh from Long Equity Partners (page 14) both pushed for more disclosure citing peer Titan Engineering (TEAL) as a comparator. MD acknowledged feedback: "Got it. So, feedback taken on the presentation, we will work on that going forward."

What management *did* say:
- Both Hi-Tech and Engineering verticals grew.
- Hi-Tech covers semiconductor, solar, consumer electronics, industrial electronics.
- Within Hi-Tech, work spans vision systems, robotics, laser, testing equipment.
- ER&D revenue was flat-to-down in Q2 — explained as "fixed bid projects awaiting acceptance"; recovery expected Q3 onwards.

## 4. Margin drivers / pressures

**Drivers**
- Operating leverage from DLM ramp on largely fixed cost base.
- Improved product mix (consumer + semicap higher value).
- Industrial AI / digital layer used internally to lift yield and throughput.

**Pressures (called out by analyst Meet from Niveshaay, page 5)**
- Gross-margin compression noted: DLM mix is higher CoGS, lower GM than pure services.
- Service revenue actually declined in Q2 → ER&D capacity diverted to DLM design support.

> "We are very consciously using some of our ER&D capability to drive the DLM. That is where we see more… So, for us, priority is if we need capacity to drive DLM, we will use our ER&D capacity to drive our DLM." [MD, page 12] — explicit prioritisation.

No specific margin guidance: "Range also, we will not be able to share. But let us say that the last 3 quarters, the last few quarters, are quite representative." [MD, page 5]

## 8. Analyst Q&A — top 3 sharp questions

### Q1 — Tej (Diamond Capital, page 16): "What is top-1 / top-3 customer concentration?"
**MD answer:** "60% is from 10 customers. That is what we can share. Individually, number 1 or number 3 or the top 3 will be difficult."
**Verdict:** Partial answer — gave aggregate top-10 (60%) but refused top-1/3. AR FY25 had already disclosed 2 customers = 39.4% so the call number is consistent and arguably more diluted.

### Q2 — Anant Jain (page 11): On disclosure quality
> "I just don't know how can one appreciate the work that we are doing if we can't even give the level of details which are expected? …on this CAPEX of Rs. 500 crores and Rs. 300 crores that you have announced, can you share in terms of segments or rather phases, when can we see these different phases…?"

**MD answer:** "Got it. So, feedback taken on the presentation, we will work on that going forward… they will definitely be in phases. We will be probably doing it across 3 phases. And as each phase is implemented, the revenue will be accredited."
**Verdict:** Acknowledged disclosure gap, committed to improvement, gave phasing in principle but no dates. **Promise to track in Q3FY26 call.**

### Q3 — Anand Trivedi (Nepean Capital, page 16): On capex financing equity component
> "Given the current cash flows in the size of your company, it is a very large number. Can you talk a bit about what is the means that you are thinking about financing this?"

**MD answer:** "It would be a combination of debt, accrual, incentives, and some equity, which we are working on… We have a broad understanding. Depending on the phases, we will decide how we want to do this."
**Verdict:** Most material question of the call. Answer is **LOW conviction** on equity timing/quantum — directly impacts existing shareholder dilution risk.

## 9. Key takeaway (2 sentences)

ASM's first-ever investor call confirmed that the DLM pivot is real and accelerating (171% Q2 growth, DLM now 63% of mix, 80-85% utilisation, 18-24 month visibility) but management is uncomfortable with the disclosure expectations of a public-market growth story — no quantitative guidance, no customer split, no segment-wise revenue, and no clarity yet on the equity component of a ~₹760 Cr capex roadmap. The next two concalls (Q3FY26 — Jan 2026, Q4FY26 — May 2026) should be judged primarily on whether (a) ER&D growth resumes as promised, (b) capacity utilisation stays >80% as new facilities come on, and (c) the equity-vs-debt mix for the announced capex is finally disclosed.
