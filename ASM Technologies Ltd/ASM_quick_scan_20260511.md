## Step 1 — Annual Report 12-Point Analysis

### 1. What does the company do? (1 paragraph)
ASM Technologies is a ~30-year-old Bengaluru-based engineering services + manufacturing company. It runs two verticals: **Engineering R&D (ER&D)** — embedded systems, software, mechanical, electronics design — and **Design-Led Manufacturing (DLM)** — low-volume, high-precision systems and sub-systems for semiconductor equipment, consumer electronics equipment, industrial electronics and solar equipment. The story for the past 8 years has been a deliberate pivot from pure ER&D into capital-intensive DLM, using its own engineering teams to design products that the same company then manufactures for global OEMs (the so-called "system design house" model). [AR FY25 pages 8, 16; Concall Nov 2025 page 2]

### 2. Business segment & revenue mix (FY26 consolidated)
- **DLM:** 53.8% of FY26 revenue (Q2FY26 was 63%, full-year settled at ~54%)
- **ER&D / Services:** 46.2%

The DLM share rose from ~12% of H1FY25 to ~58% in H1FY26 (per Nov-25 concall), a structural shift, not a one-off. [Q4FY26 presentation slide 6]

### 3. Geography mix (FY26 consolidated)
- **Domestic:** 80.0%
- **Exports:** 20.0%

Geographically the customer book is global (US, UK, Singapore, Japan, Thailand, Mexico, Vietnam) but management says global OEMs increasingly transact with their Indian entities → "domestic" billing rises even when the end-equipment ships overseas. [Q4FY26 slide 6; Concall page 15]

### 4. Customer concentration
- **39.4% of FY25 standalone revenue from just 2 customers** (FY24: 42.4% from 1 customer). [AR FY25 note 34, page 108]
- Top 10 customers ≈ **60% of FY26 revenue** (management on Q2FY26 concall, page 16).
- Management refuses to name customers or split between Hi-Tech vs Engineering on concalls.

This is **high concentration** — flag.

### 5. Promoter / management background and pledging
- **Promoter holding: 57.10%** as of 31 Mar 2025; the rest is essentially retail (resident individuals ~36.5%, only 1 FPI). No institutional float of note. [AR FY25 page 63]
- **No pledging disclosed.** Search of AR yielded no encumbrance references.
- Managing Director **Rabindra Srikantan** (founder) — 35+ yrs experience, MS Computer Engineering, USA. Currently Chairman, CII Karnataka (2025-26). Promoter family includes wife Preeti Rabindra (non-executive director) and sons Akhil and Nikhil Rabindra (listed as relatives of director).
- Chairman **M.R. Vikram** is a Fellow CA with 44 yrs experience; also on Facebook India and Chettinad Cement boards.
- Board has 4 independent directors and 1 woman director (Preeti, but non-independent).

### 6. Capex cycle status — **AGGRESSIVE EXPANSION**
ASM is in the middle of the largest capex cycle in its history:
- **FY26 capex visible in cashflow: ₹165.6 Cr net cash used in investing.** [Slide 7 Q4FY26]
- **3 new manufacturing facilities commissioned in FY26** — 2 in Bengaluru, 1 in Vietnam. Total now 7 facilities.
- **MoU with Karnataka:** ₹510 Cr over 3 phases for ESDM/DLM expansion.
- **MoU with Tamil Nadu:** ₹250 Cr DLM capacity expansion.
- **ECMS scheme approval (Mar 2026):** ₹565 Cr investment cleared for high-precision capital equipment manufacturing.
- 10-acre Karnataka land secured; construction not yet begun.
- Aggregate announced capex roadmap = **~₹1,300–1,500 Cr over 3-5 yrs** vs current consolidated net-block of only ~₹93 Cr — this is a multi-bagger of the asset base.

## Step 4 — DuPont (3-yr ROE Decomposition)

ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier

| Year | Net Margin | Asset T/O | Eq. Multiplier | ROE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY24 | -3.5% | 1.05× | 1.76× | -6.5% (reported -7.0%) |
| FY25 | 8.7% | 1.20× | 1.76× | 18.4% (reported 10.6%* — uses average equity) |
| FY26 | 11.5% | 1.05× | 1.64× | 19.8% (reported 25.7%* — uses average equity) |

*Company-reported ROE figures use opening equity in some years; using simple year-end equity gives the DuPont reconciliation above.

**Take-away:** the ROE improvement is being driven by **margin expansion** (Net margin tripling), with asset turnover essentially flat and leverage slightly falling. This is the **highest-quality form of ROE expansion** — operating leverage and mix shift, not financial engineering.

Screener.in reports higher ROCE (37.6%) and ROE (37.5%) — that uses TTM ending Q4FY26 with average capital, which spikes when equity nearly doubles within the year. The company's own 22.6%/25.7% (slide 24) is the conservative reading. **>10% divergence flag noted — for valuation use Screener; for diligence the company-reported numbers are more representative.**

## Step 5 — Bear Case (5 hard bullets)

1. **Customer concentration is dangerously high.** Two customers = 39% of FY25 revenue; top 10 = 60% of FY26 revenue. Management refuses to disclose names or split, citing NDAs. A single semicap OEM cancelling a programme in 2026-27 could erase 15-20% of revenue overnight, and ASM has no leverage in pricing renewals.

2. **Valuation has run far ahead of execution.** Market cap ~₹5,140 Cr on FY26 PAT of ₹60.8 Cr → ~85× TTM P/E. For comparison: TCS trades ~28×, KPIT ~52×, Cyient ~25×, Avalon Tech ~50×. ASM has done 1 year of clean execution. A single quarter of disappointment could de-rate the stock 40-50%.

3. **Massive capex roadmap (~₹1,500 Cr) on a ~₹100 Cr EBITDA base.** Funding is "combination of accrual, debt, incentives and some equity" — management has been deliberately vague on the equity quantum in Q2 concall. That implies a 20-40% further dilution risk over 24-36 months at potentially lower prices. Execution risk on commissioning 3 large new sites simultaneously is real.

4. **Related-party noise.** ₹11 Cr salary advance to MD (recurring), ₹33 Cr inter-corp loans to RV Forms & Gears LLP, ₹26 Cr receivable from Singapore subsidiary unchanged, large promoter warrant issuance at fixed price prior to a re-rating — all defensible individually, but cumulatively a small-cap governance watch. Standalone CFO has been **negative for 2 consecutive years** (FY24: -6 Cr; FY25: -235 Cr) — only consolidation cleans it up.

5. **DLM margins may not be repeatable.** Q4FY26 EBITDA margin was only 18.7% (vs 22.2% in Q4FY25); H2 was supported by operating leverage on a small revenue base. As 7 facilities ramp, gestation losses and depreciation rise. The 19.1% FY26 EBITDA margin may compress 200-400 bps in FY27. If revenue growth slows to 30% but margins compress, FY27 PAT could grow only 10-15%, leaving an 85× P/E orphaned.

## Step 6 — Verdict

### **WATCH (revisit on trigger)**

Three sentences:
- **(1)** ASM has executed a genuine, structural pivot from sub-scale ER&D to DLM that is now showing in the financials — 83% revenue growth, ROCE crossing 22%, CFO turning positive, debt fully covered — and it is plugged into the right tailwinds (semicap localisation, ECMS, ESDM PLI, friend-shoring).
- **(2)** However, valuation at ~85× FY26 earnings already prices in 3-4 more years of 40%+ growth, customer concentration is high, and a ~₹1,500 Cr capex programme will require both debt and probably equity dilution that has not been quantified.
- **(3)** This is a WATCH, not a BUY — wait for either (a) a 30-40% valuation reset, (b) clear disclosure of customer mix and the equity component of capex, or (c) two more clean quarters that prove FY26 was not a one-customer-order-driven blowout.
