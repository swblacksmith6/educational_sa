# Netweb Technologies India Ltd — Quick Scan
**Ticker:** NSE: NETWEB / BSE: 543945 | **Scan date:** 16 May 2026 | **CMP:** ₹3,888 | **M-cap:** ₹22,250 cr
**Latest filings reviewed:** AR FY25, Q4 FY26 concall (4-May-26), CRISIL rating reaffirmation Aug-25.

---

## 1. What does the company do?

> _For a first-time reader_ — Netweb designs and manufactures **high-end computing systems** (HCS): the racks of powerful servers that universities, defence labs, the government and large enterprises use for AI training, supercomputing simulations and private-cloud data centres. Think of them as the **"Indian Dell/Supermicro for AI infra"** — they take chips from NVIDIA, AMD and Intel, design the motherboard and chassis around them in-house, manufacture in Faridabad, and ship full-stack solutions (hardware + software + services). They are an official NVIDIA OEM partner and one of the very few Indian companies on the global Top-500 supercomputer list (15 of their systems have ranked there).

## 2. Business segments — FY26 revenue mix

| Segment | FY26 share | FY26 YoY growth | What it is |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **AI Systems & Workstations** | **43.4%** | **+459.6%** | NVIDIA Blackwell / Hopper-based GPU servers; large Tyrone Camarero AI platforms |
| **HPC / Supercomputing** | ~24% | ~+31% | CPU-heavy clusters for academic research, ISRO, defence labs, oil & gas |
| **Private Cloud / HCI** | ~24% | ~steady | Data-centre infra for enterprises (Infosys, TCS, Yotta, Zoho) |
| Storage, DC servers, Software | ~8% | mixed | Storage arrays, plain servers, software & services (non-core) |

Two years ago AI was 5% of revenue. Today it is the engine. This is the single most important structural fact about Netweb.

## 3. Geography mix
**>95% India** (domestic). Exports remain at ~5% — management has explicitly chosen to satisfy domestic demand first before pursuing exports. Customer mix is **~50% Government / ~50% Enterprise**.

## 4. Customer concentration & marquee names
Top customers: **IIT system, ISRO, Railtel, University of Delhi, Yotta, Infosys, TCS, Zoho.** 81% of revenue is repeat-customer revenue (FY25); top-10 customer relationships average 4.8 years. CRISIL noted "diversified clientele across geographies" as a positive in the August 2025 rating note. The ₹1,734 cr IndiaAI Mission strategic order (announced Sep-25) introduces near-term concentration risk — see Bear Case.

## 5. Promoter & management
**Lodha family** — Sanjay (CMD, 28% pre-OFS), Navin, Niraj, Vivek. The four collectively sold 20.79 lakh shares (3.65% stake) in Feb-26 to improve trading liquidity. **Promoter holding now 66.98%** (vs 70.64% earlier). No share pledge. Promoters have a 12-month lock-in commitment after the Feb-26 sale.

## 6. Capex cycle
**Capability-driven, not capacity-driven** — management quote: "Our capacity utilisation is 65-70%; we are a technology company, not a volume company." A new 15,000 sq ft SMT-based liquid-cooled rack facility was commissioned in FY26. **No major capex planned for FY27** — only routine ₹20–25 cr per year. This is unusual and important: most peers (Dixon, Kaynes, Syrma) are in heavy capex cycles. Netweb's growth is largely **working-capital-funded, not capex-funded**.

## 7. Working capital — _the single most-watched metric for this company_

> _Explainer for a first-time reader_ —
> - **Debtor days (receivable days)** = avg days a customer takes to pay Netweb. Lower is better. 30–60 days is normal for B2C, 80–120 for B2G in India.
> - **Inventory days** = avg days raw material / finished goods sit on the shelf. Lower = faster turnover = better.
> - **Cash conversion cycle (CCC)** = Inventory days + Debtor days − Payable days. It tells you **how many days of cash the company has tied up between paying its suppliers and getting paid by its customers**. A 100-day CCC means the company self-finances 100 days of working capital, which is risky in a high-growth phase.

| Metric | FY24 | FY25 | Q3 FY26 (Dec'25) | Q4 FY26 (Mar'26) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Receivable days | ~93 | 115 | 114 | **86** ✅ improving |
| Inventory days | ~76 | ~71 | 60 | **86** ⚠️ rising (stocking for strategic orders) |
| Cash conversion cycle | ~95 | ~100 | ~105 | **84** |
| Total borrowings (₹ cr) | 9.6 | 8.0 | ~150 | **270** ⚠️ |

Management guides CCC will stay in **90–110 day band** through FY27. The ₹270 cr short-term borrowings are explicitly to fund inventory and execution of the ₹1,734 cr IndiaAI order — management calls it "transitionary". Watch this in Q1 FY27.

## 8. Debt position & interest coverage

> _Explainer_ — **Interest coverage** = EBIT / Interest expense. Above 5x is healthy. Below 2x is a red flag.

- **Net cash: ₹83 cr** as of Mar'26 (down from ₹162 cr Mar'25 because of WC borrowings).
- Long-term borrowings: practically nil (₹13 cr leases).
- FY25 interest coverage: **>34x** (per CRISIL rationale).
- **CRISIL rating: A / Stable / A1** (upgraded from A-/A2+ in June 2025). Bank lines enhanced from ₹124 cr to **₹260 cr** in Aug-25 to support working capital.

## 9. Key risks disclosed by management
1. **Component supply chain** — NVIDIA Blackwell GPU allocation is tight globally; any allocation hit = revenue hit.
2. **Single-vendor partner dependency** on NVIDIA — though AMD and Intel partnerships exist as alternates.
3. **FX exposure** — USD trade payables of ~₹1,082 mn (unhedged a meaningful portion); Q4 FY26 had ₹52 mn MTM losses.
4. **Government dependency** — ~50% of revenue from government / quasi-government; payment cycles can be lumpy.
5. **Margin pressure** — components price volatility on AI chips; management guides 13–14% EBITDA margin (vs 14.7% FY25).

## 10. Related-party transactions
Disclosed at arm's length in the AR25 (Note 38); none classified as "material" under SEBI LODR. CSR spend of ₹12.9 mn was fully deployed. No qualifications from auditor **S S Kothari Mehta & Co. LLP**.

## 11. Auditor & qualifications
Auditor S S Kothari Mehta & Co. LLP — **clean opinion, no qualifications** in either FY24 or FY25. No auditor change in last 3 years.

## 12. Strategy stated by MD
*"India's only full-stack domestic provider for high-end computing systems… anchored on three pillars: HPC, Private Cloud, AI."* Stated FY27 revenue growth guidance: **35–40% (organic, ex-strategic orders)**, EBITDA margin **13–14%**, no major capex. Sovereign AI infra is positioned as the multi-year tailwind.

## Piotroski F-Score (FY25 vs FY24)

> _Explainer for a first-time reader_ — The **Piotroski F-Score (0–9)** is a 9-test checklist invented by Stanford prof Joseph Piotroski to gauge financial strength. Each test passed = 1 point. **7–9 = strong**, 4–6 = average, 0–3 = weak. The tests cover profitability (4), leverage/liquidity (3) and operating efficiency (2).

| # | Test | FY25 vs FY24 | Pass? |
|---|---|---|:---:|
| 1 | Positive Net Income | Yes (+₹114.5 cr) | ✅ 1 |
| 2 | Positive Operating Cash Flow | **No (-₹13.2 cr)** | ❌ 0 |
| 3 | ROA improving YoY | Slight decline (revenue grew faster than profit) | ❌ 0 |
| 4 | CFO > Net Income | No | ❌ 0 |
| 5 | Lower long-term debt YoY | Yes (8.0 vs 9.6) | ✅ 1 |
| 6 | Higher current ratio YoY | No (2.33 vs 3.13 — payables rose) | ❌ 0 |
| 7 | No new shares issued | Yes (only ESOP, IPO was prior year) | ✅ 1 |
| 8 | Higher gross margin YoY | Slight decline (14.7% vs 15.8%) | ❌ 0 |
| 9 | Higher asset turnover YoY | Yes (22.7x vs 16.2x) | ✅ 1 |

**FY25 Piotroski score: 4 / 9.** This is **lower than what a buy-quality investor wants (≥7)**. The score is depressed largely because of working-capital strain during hyper-growth (tests 2, 4, 6 all reflect WC build). **FY26 score should rebound to ~6–7** based on management commentary on CFO recovery to ₹172 cr.

| FY23 | 10.5% | 1.5x | 1.9x | **29.5%** |

**Read:** ROE rebounded sharply in FY26 because **asset turnover doubled** — every rupee of capital now generates more revenue (operating leverage in a fixed-asset-light, design-led model). Net margin compressed slightly as AI segment carries lower gross margins than HPC but is being offset by scale.

## Bear Case (5 bullets, intentionally aggressive)

1. **Customer concentration is the elephant in the room.** The single ₹1,734 cr IndiaAI Mission order represents ~80% of all of FY26 revenue. Execution of one deal across Q4 FY26–Q2 FY27 means a slippage of one quarter would devastate consensus estimates.
2. **Working capital is masquerading as growth.** FY25 CFO was negative; FY26 borrowings spiked to ₹270 cr from near-zero. The business is **funding inventory and receivables through short-term debt**, and any disruption (component allocation, GoI payment delay, FX move) compresses liquidity fast.
3. **Margin ceiling, not floor.** Management has been **lowering** EBITDA margin guidance (15.8% FY23 → 14.7% FY25 → 13–14% FY27). AI hardware is fundamentally a lower-margin business than HPC because chip cost is 60–70% of BOM and NVIDIA captures most of the value — Netweb is the integrator, not the IP holder.
4. **Valuation has zero margin of safety.** TTM P/E of 108x, EV/EBITDA of 82x. Even on FY28E (assuming 35%+ growth holds), forward P/E is ~50x — still demanding. Peer Dixon trades at 38x, Kaynes 70x. A single missed quarter compresses the multiple by 30%+.
5. **Geopolitical / chip allocation risk.** Sovereign AI is a great theme **only if Blackwell GPUs continue to flow into India in volume**. US export-control noise on advanced semis to non-aligned countries is a non-zero risk. Netweb has no captive silicon, no IP.

## Verdict

**WATCH — high-conviction watchlist, await better entry / Q1 FY27 walk-the-talk.**

The business is one of the cleanest plays on India's sovereign-AI infra capex with genuine technical capability, debt-free B/S, marquee customer list and a generational tailwind from IndiaAI Mission. **However**, (a) the working-capital build needs to convert to operating cash in FY27, (b) margin trajectory needs to stabilise at 13%+, and (c) current 108x TTM PE prices in flawless execution. A 20–25% drawdown OR confirmation of CFO/PAT > 0.8 in H1 FY27 would convert this to BUY-WORTHY.
