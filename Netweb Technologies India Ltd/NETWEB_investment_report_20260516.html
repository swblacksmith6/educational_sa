Working Capital Cycle — The Most-Watched Block

Beginner explainer —
• Debtor / Receivable days = average days customers take to pay. Lower is better. 30–60 = B2C norm; 80–120 = B2G norm in India.
• Inventory days = average days raw material / finished goods sit on the shelf. Lower = faster turnover.
• Payable days = average days the company takes to pay its suppliers.
• Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) = Inventory days + Debtor days − Payable days. It tells you how many days of cash the business has tied up between paying suppliers and getting paid by customers. A 100-day CCC means the company is self-financing 3+ months of working capital — risky in a hyper-growth phase.

Sector-Specific Valuation — Why Not Just P/E

Different multiples answer different questions —
• P/E: simplest, but assumes margin stability.
• EV/EBITDA: strips out capital structure and depreciation — better for hardware / manufacturing companies with different debt & capex profiles.
• EV/Sales: useful when margins are still normalising; tells you what you pay per ₹ of revenue.
• PEG (P/E ÷ growth): <1 = cheap; >2 = expensive. Adjusts P/E for the growth rate.
• EV/Order Book: sector-specific — useful for capital-goods / EMS firms because order book = forward revenue visibility.
Netweb sits at the intersection of (a) Indian EMS, (b) global AI infrastructure OEM, and (c) defence-electronics — each lens gives a different read.
