*As of 16-May-2026 | Inputs: Screener.in, BSE/NSE filings, MarketsMojo, Yahoo Finance*

## Why look beyond P/E for Netweb

> _Explainer for first-time readers_ — Different valuation multiples answer different questions:
> - **P/E (Price to Earnings)** is the simplest — how many years of current earnings the stock costs. Works well for mature, stable-margin businesses.
> - **EV/EBITDA (Enterprise Value to EBITDA)** strips out capital structure (debt/cash) and non-cash charges (depreciation). **Better for hardware/manufacturing companies with different debt levels and capex cycles**, because it compares operating profitability directly.
> - **EV/Sales (or P/S)** is used when a business is in rapid scaling phase and margins are still normalising. It tells you how much you pay per rupee of revenue.
> - **PEG (P/E to Growth)** divides PE by expected earnings-growth rate. PEG < 1 = cheap relative to growth; PEG > 2 = expensive.
> - **EV/Order Book** is sector-specific — useful for capital-goods / EMS firms because order book = forward revenue visibility.
>
> Netweb sits at the intersection of three sub-sectors: (a) **Indian EMS / contract manufacturing** (Dixon, Kaynes, Syrma, Avalon, Cyient DLM), (b) **AI infrastructure plays** (global: Supermicro, Dell ISG, HPE), and (c) **Defence / dual-use electronics** (Data Patterns, BEL). For each lens, use a different multiple.

## Lens 4 — Forward-looking PEG and EV/Order-book

**PEG Ratio** (TTM P/E ÷ expected EPS growth FY27E)
- Management FY27 guidance: 35–40% organic + strategic order execution. Implied EPS growth FY27E ~ **45–55%**.
- PEG = 108 ÷ 50 = **~2.2x**.
- By the "PEG < 1 is cheap, > 2 is expensive" rule of thumb, Netweb is **expensive but not absurdly so** for a hyper-growth name. Compare: Data Patterns PEG ~3.5, Kaynes ~2.5, Dixon ~1.5.

**EV/Order Book** — order-book based valuation:
- Total order book + strategic backlog + L1 = **₹2,400 cr**; M-cap (= EV given net cash) ≈ ₹22,250 cr.
- **EV/Order Book = 9.3x** — this is rich vs EMS peers (Kaynes ~3x, Cyient DLM ~1.4x) but reflects (a) order book turns ~once every 4 months at current run-rate (i.e. the order book is short-cycle, not a 2-3 year backlog like defence), and (b) the pipeline of ₹4,400 cr is not yet in order book.
- **If we include 60% pipeline conversion (₹2,640 cr) → adjusted EV/Order Book ≈ 4.4x** — more reasonable but still demanding.

## Lens 5 — Reverse-DCF / scenario-based valuation

> _Explainer_ — A reverse DCF asks: "what growth rate is the current stock price implying?" If the implied rate is unrealistic, the stock is overpriced; if achievable, it's defensible.

Using a simple 10-year reverse DCF with **15% cost of equity**, **3% terminal growth** and **9.3% PAT margin held flat**, the current ₹22,250 cr market cap implies:
- **~30% revenue CAGR for the next 10 years**.

This is approximately what management is guiding for the **first 2 years (35–40%)**, with a gradual taper. **The price embeds management hitting the upper end of guidance for half a decade.** Possible, but not the base case.

## Bottom line — which multiple matters most for Netweb?

For a high-growth, debt-free, working-capital-intensive Indian OEM with sovereign exposure, the most useful triangulation is:

1. **EV/EBITDA** (37x peer median × 1.3x premium for ROCE leadership = ~48x fair) — Netweb at **82x is ~70% above fair value on this lens**.
2. **PEG** (2.0x is the "fair" line for Indian small-cap high-growth) — Netweb at **2.2x is borderline fair** if execution holds.
3. **EV/Sales** (peer median ~4x × premium = 5–6x fair) — Netweb at **10.2x is the most stretched lens**.

**Synthesis:** The stock is **priced for perfection on EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales**, but is **defensible on PEG** if FY27–28 deliver 40%+ EPS growth. A **15–25% drawdown** would bring multiples into a more comfortable zone; conversely, a **single missed quarter** could compress the multiple by 30–40% (as happened to Kaynes Q4 FY26 — stock fell 18% on a PAT miss).

> **Best entry framework:** Stagger entries on EV/EBITDA correction to <60x, or PE correction to <85x, with a target weight tilted by execution evidence (CFO/PAT in H1 FY27 above 0.8, borrowings unwinding below ₹150 cr).
